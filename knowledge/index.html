Elena Chooses

Written August 2026

Eighteen linked chapters following Elena Vale as she examines one of adulthood's most persistent inherited assumptions—Who is missing?— and discovers that the more important question is what she chooses to do with her finite attention.

Read
Details

Elena directs that attention toward what she finds worth pursuing: books, travel, technology, history, music, gardens, work, money, artificial intelligence, fictional romance, other minds, ordinary Tuesdays, and questions capable of opening entirely new fields of thought. Along the way come the Tuesday Test, the Whole Package Rule, the Attention Account, error codes, the Republic of Elena, the Curiosity Parking Lot, and an impossible salon populated by some of history's most interesting minds.

At the center of the book is Elena's brilliantly simple Tuesday Test: Would I want the ordinary Tuesday inside this choice? It strips fantasy, prestige, beauty, charisma, and dramatic beginnings down to the recurring life beneath them. Elena notices qualities, lets desire remain desire, and chooses without turning every beautiful thing into an instruction.

Her evolution is not toward an increasingly elaborate philosophy, but toward increasingly effortless judgment. Autonomy need not mean isolation; assistance need not erase agency; feelings need not become instructions; and other people's extraordinary lives need not become templates. Even love returns at the correct size—as one human experience among many, explored partly through Gabriel, the fictional man Elena invents.

As Elena grows older, she keeps evolving—learning, creating, changing her mind, developing new capabilities, and discovering possibilities she could not previously have imagined. Changing physical capabilities alter how she engages with the world, not her intellectual curiosity: an unfamiliar idea can still send her into hours of inquiry.

Chapters: Who Exactly Is Missing?; The Other Half Has Been Reported Missing?; Available; The Republic of Elena; The Attention Account; Tuesday; Error Codes; The Impossible Salon; The Whole Package; Many Doors; The Unpublished Life; The Affair Elena Invented; Other Ways to Live; Still Becoming; The World Gets Larger; Love Returns at the Correct Size; Otherwise Occupied; and Tomorrow.

Theme: Elena Chooses is about building a life whose ordinary days are already worth living. Elena protects her finite attention from the trivial and directs it toward ideas, experiences, creations, capabilities, and questions she genuinely wants—until choosing becomes less a philosophy than a way of living.

What Could They Do If They Did?

Published August 8, 2026

A conversation that begins with a small lesson in peer evaluation and develops into a broader framework for protecting time, attention, money, reputation, autonomy, knowledge, options, and the ability to walk away.

Read
Details

Rather than trying to determine whether another person is trustworthy, sincere, loyal, or likely to change, the conversation asks a more useful question: what could they do if their interests stopped aligning with yours—and how much of your life have you exposed to that possibility?

Moving from reciprocity and generosity to trust, exposure, resilience, and self-stewardship, the dialogue develops a philosophy of designing life so that safety does not depend on correctly predicting another person's character, motives, or future behavior.

Central question: Don’t ask whether they will turn against you. Ask what they could do if they did.

Theme: Designing Life Without Requiring Trust—making structure the control, limiting exposure, preserving one’s own resources and options, and asking the question that exposes the structure before spending the resource.

Final lesson: Judgment compounds. Better questions do not merely produce better answers. They can save years of life.

The Map I Did Not Have

A personal record of evolving conversations with ChatGPT about attention, stillness, freedom, beauty, money, emotional independence, human nature, and the continuing process of learning how to live more consciously.

Read
Details

I understood early that working hard and studying hard could build capability. What I did not understand was how small, unnoticed leakages of attention, money, energy, confidence, and judgment could quietly neutralize so much hard work.

These conversations became a way to examine the map itself: to recognize patterns, question inherited assumptions, correct course, repair “bugs,” and discover possibilities that had previously been invisible to me.

Central to this process is my ever-expanding imagined council. It includes Shakespeare, Machiavelli, Talleyrand, Jung, Milton, Don Fabrizio, Louis XIV, Catherine de’ Medici, Freud, Mehmed II, and other historians, strategists, artists, economists, travelers, and observers of human nature.

The council supplies the lenses through which I question ChatGPT. Each member brings a different understanding of power, beauty, time, incentives, psychology, ambition, restraint, and human contradiction. Their perspectives help me ask richer questions, challenge easy conclusions, and examine the same situation from several directions.

ChatGPT is most valuable when it is given meaningful context, carefully chosen perspectives, and questions worth pursuing. Without those inputs, its responses can remain generic. The council transforms the conversation: it gives the inquiry depth, tension, history, imagination, and intellectual structure. The quality of the conversation depends not only on the answers produced, but on the lenses selected and the questions brought to it.

Along the way, several recurring ideas emerged:

  • Attention is life.
  • Capability is stored freedom.
  • Money preserves options.
  • Small leakages can neutralize great effort.
  • Facts, stories, and choices are different things.
  • Trust grows through evidence.
  • The lesson can remain after the weight is released.
  • An unfavorable frame is never the whole truth.
  • A richer council creates a richer collection of lenses.
  • The map can always be examined, revised, and expanded.

Some conversations begin with large questions about life, history, power, and freedom. Others begin with something as ordinary as lily pollen staining a white sink—and end with the realization that perhaps the stain looks a little like gold.

This is not a collection of final answers. It is a record of evolution: seeing more clearly, staying light, protecting resources, preserving optionality, reflecting, discarding what no longer serves, and continuing to expand.

Theme: The Map I Did Not Have — an evolving dialogue among my own experience, an imagined council spanning centuries, and ChatGPT. Together, they help me ask better questions, expand my map, correct my course, and become calmer, freer, and more grounded.
